# Go-To-Market And Pricing

This document turns the engineering work into a simple sellable offer for diagnostic labs.

## Positioning

Sell this as:

`Diagnostic lab management + patient portal + billing + reports + multi-branch operations`

Do not lead with "multi-tenant SaaS" in sales conversations.
Lead with business outcomes:

- faster billing
- cleaner test/report workflow
- patient portal access
- multi-branch control
- WhatsApp/email reminders
- branded reports

## Ideal First Customers

Start with:

1. single-location labs with 3-15 staff
2. labs with one main lab plus a few collection centers
3. owner-led businesses that still run parts of billing/reporting manually

Do not start with:

1. enterprise hospital chains
2. labs requiring custom LIS/HIS integrations on day 1
3. customers demanding on-premise deployment first

## Recommended Product Packages

Keep the first offer simple.

### 1. Starter

Best for:

- one lab
- small team
- basic patient portal and billing

Include:

- 1 organization
- 1 lab
- up to 10 staff users
- patient management
- test management
- invoices and payments
- patient portal
- PDF reports
- email notifications
- basic WhatsApp reminders

Do not include:

- custom domain
- advanced white-labeling
- dedicated support SLA
- custom integrations

### 2. Growth

Best for:

- labs with branches or collection centers
- owner + operations manager usage

Include everything in Starter, plus:

- multiple labs / branches
- collection centers
- inventory
- doctor referrals
- visitor logs / analytics
- custom roles
- branded public pages
- custom domain
- organization-specific payment/email/WhatsApp config

This should be your default sales package.

### 3. Premium

Best for:

- larger diagnostic groups
- customers wanting white-label and higher support

Include everything in Growth, plus:

- priority onboarding
- data import help
- report-template customization
- higher support priority
- optional dedicated deployment
- optional custom integrations

## Pricing Model

Use a simple monthly SaaS fee plus onboarding.

Recommended commercial structure:

### Starter

- monthly subscription
- one-time onboarding/setup fee

### Growth

- higher monthly subscription
- onboarding/setup fee
- optional per-branch add-on if needed

### Premium

- custom pricing
- implementation fee
- optional annual contract

## Recommended Early Pricing Logic

Do not over-optimize price before 5-10 customers.

Use this logic:

1. charge setup separately
2. charge monthly based on business size, not tiny feature differences
3. upsell branches, custom domain, imports, and premium support

Good first commercial structure:

- one-time setup fee
- monthly platform fee
- optional branch add-on
- optional import/migration fee
- optional custom integration fee

## What To Charge For Separately

Keep these as paid add-ons:

- data migration from Excel/old software
- custom report templates
- custom domain setup
- WhatsApp/Meta setup help
- payment gateway setup help
- staff training
- dedicated hosting
- custom integrations

## Recommended Sales Motion

### Phase 1

Use founder-led sales.

Flow:

1. demo
2. collect requirements
3. provision tenant
4. configure branding/settings
5. import basic data
6. train owner/admin
7. go live

### Phase 2

Once 3-5 customers are live:

1. standardize onboarding
2. reduce custom work
3. make pricing more fixed
4. build simple sales collateral

## Minimum Viable Commercial Offer

If you want to start selling quickly, your first offer can be:

`We set up your lab-branded system with patient management, test workflow, billing, patient portal, branded reports, and reminders in a few days.`

That is enough to close early customers.

## What Not To Promise Yet

Until staging and production rollout are fully proven, do not promise:

1. heavy third-party integrations
2. enterprise SLA
3. on-prem support
4. full self-serve onboarding with no assistance
5. advanced analytics as the main selling point

## Conversion Strategy

Use this order:

1. IRM becomes proof the system works in production
2. onboard one second customer manually
3. document common onboarding steps
4. turn onboarding into a semi-standard service
5. then market it as a real product

## Simple Offer Summary

What you are really selling:

- software subscription
- onboarding/setup service
- optional white-labeling
- optional migration and support

That is the correct way to monetize this in the first phase.
